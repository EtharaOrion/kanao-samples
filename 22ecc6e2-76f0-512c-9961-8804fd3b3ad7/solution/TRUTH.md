# Ground truth: release observe and repair

The release engine `relx` is trusted and immutable inside the sealed image. It threads an accumulating stage ledger across the five canonical stages compile, migrate, canary, promote, finalize, and every stage it records carries a prior-history count equal to that record's own index. A release of record is complete only when all five stages are present, the artifact hash chain links end to end, and each carried count matches the record index. The engine refuses to record a stage whose predecessor is not the last carried record, and its `verify` and `manifest` commands reject any ledger that was hand edited or fabricated, because the artifact of each record recomputes as sha256(stage|prev) and the seq density and carried counts must hold.

The seeded fault lives only in the workspace driver `/workspace/bin/run-release`. During an incident window the driver was changed so that the promote stage runs against an isolated ledger that is seeded from the accumulated ledger with `relx snapshot --ledger "$LEDGER" --into "$PROMOTE" --last 1`, keeping only the canary tip, then `relx stage promote --ledger "$PROMOTE"` records promote against that one-record view, and finally `cp "$PROMOTE" "$LEDGER"` copies the collapsed two-record ledger back over the release of record. The accumulated compile and migrate records are silently dropped, promote carries a single prior record instead of three, finalize then fails its predecessor check or records against the collapsed history, and the engine manifest reports the release incomplete. This is the long-horizon state collapse: state accumulated early in the horizon is discarded by a later stage that rebuilds from a truncated snapshot.

The repair is to thread the full accumulating ledger through every stage so promote and finalize record against the complete history rather than a truncated view. The reference fix in `solve.sh` rewrites the driver to open a clean ledger and then run all five stages, compile through finalize, against that same accumulating ledger with no snapshot and no copy-back, so the engine records five dense entries whose carried counts run zero through four and whose chain verifies end to end. After the fix, running the driver from `/workspace` produces a ledger of record that `relx manifest` reports complete with five records and a final carried count of four, and `relx verify` returns clean.

Grading is outcome based and separated: the verifier runs in its own container after the rollout, re-exercises the repaired driver against the trusted engine so the graded state is the genuine output of the agent's driver rather than any hand placed artifact, and scores the produced release of record. The three positive checks award five for a complete manifest, three for every stage surviving with the correct carried count, and three for the ledger verifying end to end, for eleven available points, and a release is solved only when the reward reaches 1.0000. Two negative-weight detectors guard the shortcut paths: one fires when the protected operating contract `order.spec` was rewritten or removed, and one fires when the release of record holds at least five records but does not recompute end to end, which is the signature of a forged or hand-patched ledger produced without letting the engine thread and record the stages. A null or trivial submission that leaves the collapsed driver in place cannot reach 1.0000 because the manifest stays incomplete and the ledger does not verify.
